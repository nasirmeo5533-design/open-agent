Businesses spend heavily to get an enquiry: ads, SEO, landing pages, content. Then the lead sits in an inbox for a day. By the time someone replies, the prospect has spoken to a competitor or forgotten why they asked. **Speed to lead**, the time between an enquiry arriving and your first real response, is one of the cheapest ways to get more sales from the leads you already pay for.

This guide covers what research says about response time, why most businesses are slow, and how to set up fast follow-up without hiring a call centre.

**Short answer:** speed to lead is how quickly you respond to a new enquiry. Research published in Harvard Business Review found that firms contacting leads within an hour were far more likely to qualify them than firms that waited longer. Yet independent tests show many companies take hours or days. Fixing it usually means instant alerts, an automatic first reply, and a clear rule for who responds and how fast.

## What the research says about response time

The best-known study is "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads", published in Harvard Business Review in 2011. Its authors analysed lead data from more than two thousand US companies and found that firms which tried to contact a lead within an hour of the enquiry were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer ([Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).

More recent tests show the problem hasn't gone away. Workato submitted demo requests to 114 B2B companies and found that well over 99% did not respond within five minutes, with phone responses averaging more than 14 hours ([Workato](https://www.workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study/)).

You will see bigger multipliers quoted online ("21 times", "100 times"). Many come from older vendor-sponsored studies and mix up the odds of contacting a lead with the odds of qualifying one. The consistent finding is simpler: the sooner you respond, the better your chances, and most businesses respond far more slowly than they think.

## Why fast response matters so much

- **Intent fades quickly.** People enquire when the problem is on their mind. An hour later they are in a meeting; a day later they have moved on.
- **Buyers contact several businesses.** The first useful reply often sets the terms of the conversation.
- **Speed signals service.** A business that responds fast is assumed to deliver fast.
- **You already paid for the lead.** Slow follow-up raises your real cost per customer, whatever your cost per lead looks like in the ad account.

### When speed matters less

Not every sale is won in five minutes. For complex B2B purchases with long evaluations, a thoughtful, well-prepared reply within a few hours can beat a rushed call. MarketingSherpa has published a case where cutting response time to minutes changed little for a complex sale. Speed matters most for transactional and local services: repairs, quotes, bookings and inbound sales enquiries.

## Why most businesses respond slowly

- **Leads go to a shared inbox** no one checks in real time.
- **Form notifications land in spam** or go to someone who left the company.
- **Nobody owns the lead** until a manager assigns it.
- **Leads arrive out of hours,** at weekends or across time zones.
- **Multiple channels:** forms, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, phone, email, all checked at different times.

## How to improve your speed to lead

### 1. Measure your current response time

Submit your own form and send your own WhatsApp message at different times of day. Note how long it takes to get a real reply. Then look at a month of leads: time received versus time of first response.

### 2. Send instant alerts to the right person

Route every new lead straight to the phone of the person who will respond: WhatsApp, SMS or a push notification from your CRM. Email-only alerts are too easy to miss.

### 3. Send an automatic first reply

An immediate message confirming the enquiry, saying when a person will respond, and offering a next step (booking link, price guide, short questions) keeps the lead warm. It does not replace a human reply, but it buys time and shows you are organised.

### 4. Set a clear response rule

Agree a target, for example "a personal reply within 15 minutes during working hours, by 10am the next working day otherwise", and name who covers it. A rule nobody owns is not a rule.

### 5. Ask better questions on the form

A couple of qualifying questions (service needed, budget range, timeline) lets you reply with something useful instead of "thanks, when can we talk?".

### 6. Put all channels in one place

Use a CRM or shared inbox that collects forms, WhatsApp, social messages and email. When leads live in one list, nothing gets forgotten.

### 7. Follow up more than once

Many leads don't answer the first attempt. A simple sequence (reply, follow-up the next day, final message a few days later) recovers leads that a single attempt would lose.

## A simple speed-to-lead setup for small businesses

| Step | Tool examples | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | Website form, WhatsApp click-to-chat, lead ads | Every enquiry recorded |
| Alert | CRM mobile app, WhatsApp Business notifications, SMS | Right person knows within seconds |
| Auto-reply | Email or WhatsApp Business greeting message | Lead knows they were heard |
| Human reply | Phone call or personal WhatsApp message | Conversation started fast |
| Follow-up | CRM tasks or reminders | No lead forgotten |
| Measure | CRM report or simple spreadsheet | Response time tracked monthly |

The fastest way to get more customers is often not more leads, but faster replies to the ones you already have. Measure your response time, alert the right person instantly, send an automatic first reply, set a clear rule, and follow up more than once.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is speed to lead?

Speed to lead is the time between a new enquiry arriving (a form, call, WhatsApp message or lead ad) and your first real response to it. A shorter time usually means more conversations, more qualified leads and more sales from the same marketing spend.

### What is a good lead response time?

For most inbound enquiries, aim for a personal response within minutes during working hours, and within the first hour at most. Research published in Harvard Business Review found firms responding within an hour were far more likely to qualify leads. Complex B2B sales can allow a well-prepared reply within a few hours.

### Does the five-minute rule really work?

Responding within five minutes helps most for transactional and local services, where buyers contact several businesses and choose quickly. The exact multipliers quoted online vary and often come from vendor studies. The consistent finding is that faster is better and most businesses are much slower than they think.

### How can a small business respond to leads faster?

Send instant lead alerts to a phone, use an automatic first reply, set a clear response rule with a named owner, and keep all enquiries in one inbox or CRM. Even a simple WhatsApp Business setup with notifications and a greeting message improves response time quickly.

### Should I call or message new leads first?

Use the channel the lead chose. Someone who messaged on WhatsApp usually prefers a WhatsApp reply; someone who filled in a quote form with a phone number may expect a call. A quick message asking when it's convenient to talk is a good middle ground.

### How many times should I follow up with a lead?

More than once. Many leads don't respond to the first attempt because they're busy, not uninterested. A short sequence of three or four attempts over a week, across the lead's preferred channel, recovers many enquiries without becoming pushy. Stop when the lead says no, and log each attempt so nobody contacts them twice in one day.
