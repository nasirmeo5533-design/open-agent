Ask five web designers what a small business website costs and you will get five answers that differ by thousands. That is not always because someone is overcharging. The quotes usually describe different websites. The real **small business website cost** depends on how many pages you need, who writes the content, what the site has to do, and who looks after it once it is live.

This guide breaks the price into the parts you are actually paying for, so you can compare quotes properly and decide what your business needs right now.

**Short answer:** a simple brochure site from a freelancer or small agency usually costs a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, while custom design, online stores and booking or membership features push the price much higher. Expect yearly running costs too: domain, hosting or platform fees, and maintenance.

## Why website quotes vary so much

A website price is mostly time. The more decisions, pages, content and testing involved, the more hours the project needs. Two quotes for "a five-page website" can hide very different amounts of work:

- One uses a ready-made theme with your logo and colours, and you supply all the text and photos.
- The other includes a short discovery call, a custom layout, copywriting, image sourcing, basic on-page SEO, speed work and testing on several phones.

Both are honest quotes. They are simply not the same product. Before comparing prices, ask each provider to list what is included, page by page.

## The main factors that drive website cost

### Number and type of pages

A home page, services page, about page and contact page is a very different job from a site with twenty service pages, location pages and a blog. Each page needs layout, content and checking. Template pages (like blog posts) are cheaper per page than unique ones.

### Design: template or custom

A good theme on WordPress, Webflow or Shopify gives you a professional starting point at a lower cost. Custom design means someone plans the layout around your brand and your customers' questions. It costs more, and it is worth it when your market is crowded and the site has to do real selling.

### Content and copywriting

This is the cost most owners underestimate. Writing clear service pages takes time, whether you do it or someone else does. Sites often launch late because the owner is still "finishing the text". If a quote excludes copywriting, budget your own hours for it.

### Features and integrations

Contact forms are standard. Booking systems, quote calculators, payment, member areas, multilingual content and CRM integrations each add setup and testing time. An online store adds product setup, shipping and tax rules, payment gateways and checkout testing.

### SEO and performance basics

A site that loads quickly and has proper titles, headings and structured data starts in a much better position on Google. Google's own guidance on [Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) explains the loading, responsiveness and layout-stability measures it looks at. Some quotes include this groundwork; cheap ones often do not.

## Typical website price ranges by type

These are rough market ranges for freelancers and small agencies, not fixed prices. Large agencies charge more, and DIY builders cost less in money but more in your time.

| Website type | What it usually includes | Rough one-off cost |
|---|---|---|
| DIY builder site | You build it on Wix, Squarespace or similar | Mostly your time, plus monthly plan |
| Basic brochure site (4 to 6 pages) | Theme-based design, your content, contact form | About $500 to $2,500 |
| Custom small business site (6 to 15 pages) | Custom layout, copywriting help, on-page SEO, speed work | About $2,500 to $8,000 |
| Online store | Product setup, payments, shipping, store theme | About $2,000 to $10,000+ |
| Custom web application | Logins, dashboards, custom functionality | Usually $10,000+ |

Treat any figure on the internet, including ours, as a starting point for a conversation. Your quote should be based on your actual pages and features.

## Running costs: the part most quotes leave out

A website is not a one-off purchase. Plan for these every year:

- **Domain name:** usually a small yearly fee, depending on the extension.
- **Hosting or platform plan:** shared hosting for a small WordPress site is inexpensive; managed hosting, Webflow plans and Shopify plans cost more but include more. Check the provider's current pricing page, because plans change.
- **Premium themes and plugins:** many renew yearly.
- **Maintenance:** updates, backups, security checks and small content changes. Some owners do this themselves; others pay a monthly retainer.
- **Email:** business email (for example Google Workspace or Microsoft 365) is usually billed per user.

A cheap build on an expensive or fragile setup can cost more over three years than a well-built site with low running costs. Ask for the expected yearly cost, not just the build price.

## How to get an accurate quote

You will get closer, more comparable quotes if you prepare a short brief:

1. **List the pages you need** and what each page must help a visitor do.
2. **Say who will write the content** and supply photos.
3. **List the features** you need on day one, and the ones that can wait.
4. **Share two or three sites you like** and say why.
5. **Name your budget range.** It lets a provider suggest what fits instead of guessing.

Then compare quotes line by line: pages, design approach, content, SEO basics, speed, training, and what happens after launch.

## When spending more is worth it

Paying more makes sense when the website is your main source of leads or sales, when you compete with well-funded businesses, or when you will run paid ads to the site. A slow or confusing landing page wastes ad spend, because you pay for the click whether the visitor stays or not.

Spending less makes sense when you are testing a new business idea, when most customers find you through referrals, or when you need a credible online presence quickly and plan to improve it later.

## Red flags in a website quote

- No list of pages or features, just a single price.
- You will not own the domain or have admin access to the site.
- No mention of mobile testing or page speed.
- Ongoing fees that are not explained.
- Promises of "first page of Google" as part of a website build.

## Conclusion

The honest answer to "how much does a website cost" is: it depends on what the website has to do. Start from the job (more enquiries, online sales, bookings), list the pages and features that support it, and budget for running costs as well as the build.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a small business pay for a website?

Most small businesses pay somewhere between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars for a simple, professional site built by a freelancer or small agency. Custom design, copywriting, online stores and integrations raise the price. The right budget depends on how much of your revenue the website is expected to bring in.

### Is it cheaper to build a website yourself?

In money, yes. Builders like Wix or Squarespace cost a monthly fee and nothing upfront. In time, it can be expensive: planning pages, writing content, choosing images and fixing layout problems often takes owners many evenings. A DIY site works well for testing an idea; a professional build usually works better when the site must generate leads.

### What are the monthly costs of running a website?

Typical ongoing costs are hosting or a platform plan, the yearly domain renewal, business email, renewals for premium themes or plugins, and optional maintenance. A basic site can run on a small monthly amount, while ecommerce platforms and maintenance retainers cost more. Ask for the expected yearly total before you choose a platform.

### How long does it take to build a small business website?

A simple site with ready content can launch in two to four weeks. Custom design, copywriting and features like booking or payments usually take four to ten weeks. The most common delay is content, so having your text and photos ready is the fastest way to shorten the timeline.

### Does a new website include SEO?

Sometimes, but "SEO" in a website quote usually means the basics: page titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text, a sitemap and decent page speed. That groundwork matters, but ongoing SEO (content, links, technical improvements) is a separate, longer-term service. Check exactly which SEO tasks a quote covers.

### Should I choose WordPress, Webflow or Shopify?

Choose by what the site needs to do. Shopify is built for online stores. WordPress is flexible and has a huge plugin ecosystem, but needs regular updates. Webflow suits design-led sites that are easy to edit without plugins. The best platform is the one that fits your features and that you or your team can maintain.
