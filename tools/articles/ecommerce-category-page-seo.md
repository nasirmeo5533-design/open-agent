When store owners think about SEO, they usually think about product pages. But for many searches, the page Google ranks is a category page: "women's running shoes", "oak dining tables", "vegan protein powder". Shoppers searching broad terms want to compare options, and a category page shows them options. That is why **ecommerce category page SEO** is often the biggest opportunity an online store has.

This guide explains why category pages rank, how to optimise them, and how to handle filters so they don't create thousands of near-duplicate pages.

**Short answer:** category (or collection) pages match the intent of broad, high-volume shopping searches, collect internal links from your navigation, and stay live while individual products come and go. Optimise them with a clear title and heading, a short useful introduction, well-organised products, helpful buying information further down, and controlled filter URLs.

## Why category pages often outrank product pages

### They match the search intent

Someone searching "men's waterproof jackets" hasn't chosen a product yet. Google tends to show pages that let them compare: category pages, marketplace listings and buying guides. A single product page is a weaker match for that search.

### They collect internal links

Your main navigation, breadcrumbs and product pages all link to categories. That internal linking tells Google these pages are important.

### They are stable

Products sell out, get discontinued or change names. Categories stay. A category URL that has existed for years builds history and links that a short-lived product page never will.

Product pages still matter: they rank for specific searches like a model name plus "price" or "review". A healthy store needs both.

## How to optimise a category page

### 1. Choose one main keyword per category

Map each category to the search that best describes it, using the words customers actually type. "Sofas" or "couches"? "Trainers" or "sneakers"? Check search volumes and the wording your target market uses. Avoid two categories targeting the same keyword; they compete with each other.

### 2. Write a clear title tag and H1

Include the category keyword naturally: "Oak Dining Tables | Brand" as the title and "Oak Dining Tables" as the heading. Keep it plain. The title is what shoppers see in search results.

### 3. Add a short introduction above the products

Two or three sentences that confirm the shopper is in the right place and highlight what makes your range different. Keep it short above the product grid so products remain visible on mobile.

### 4. Put helpful buying information below the products

This is where most stores either do nothing or paste a block of keyword-stuffed text. Instead, answer the questions buyers ask: how to choose a size, which material suits which use, how care differs. Short sections with headings and a few FAQs are useful to shoppers and give Google more context.

### 5. Show products in a sensible order

Lead with best sellers and in-stock items. A category page whose first row is out of stock frustrates shoppers and wastes attention.

### 6. Link to subcategories and related categories

Clear links to subcategories ("Extendable oak dining tables", "Round oak dining tables") help shoppers narrow down and help Google understand your site structure.

### 7. Use structured data and breadcrumbs

Breadcrumb navigation with BreadcrumbList structured data helps Google understand where the category sits in your store. Product pages should carry Product structured data with price and availability.

## Filters and faceted navigation: the hidden SEO problem

Filters for size, colour, price and brand are essential for shoppers. But each filter combination can create a new URL, and a store with a few hundred products can generate thousands of filter URLs. Google's own [guide to faceted navigation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation) explains that crawling all these URLs wastes resources and can slow down discovery of the pages that matter.

Google describes two approaches:

- **If you don't need filtered pages in search,** prevent crawling of filter URLs, for example with robots.txt rules on the filter parameters.
- **If some filtered pages deserve to rank** (for example "black leather sofas" has real search demand), make them proper landing pages: stable URLs, a consistent parameter order, unique titles and some unique content.

A practical rule: let a filter combination become an indexable page only when people actually search for it and you have enough products to fill it.

### Shopify and WooCommerce specifics

Shopify creates collection URLs and product URLs inside collections (for example /collections/shoes/products/item). Current themes link to the canonical /products/ URL, but older or customised themes sometimes don't. Check that internal links point to the canonical product URL. On WooCommerce, filter plugins vary widely in how they build URLs, so check what your plugin generates before letting it loose on a large catalogue.

## Common category page mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No text at all | Google has little context beyond product names | Short intro plus buying guidance below products |
| Long keyword-stuffed text above products | Pushes products down, reads badly | Keep above-grid text short and useful |
| Two categories targeting one keyword | Pages compete with each other | Merge, or give each a distinct target |
| Every filter combination indexable | Thousands of thin pages, wasted crawl | Control filter URLs; index only proven demand |
| Empty or tiny categories | Thin pages with little value | Merge or hide until stocked |
| Deleting seasonal categories | Loses history and links each year | Keep the URL live and update it each season |

## How to measure category page SEO

In Google Search Console, filter the Performance report to your /collections/ or /category/ pages. Watch impressions, clicks and average position for each category's main keyword. Then connect it to revenue in your analytics: which categories bring organic visitors who actually buy.

## Conclusion

For most online stores, category pages are where SEO effort pays off fastest. Give each category one clear target, a useful introduction, real buying guidance, good internal links and controlled filters.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are category pages more important than product pages for SEO?

For broad shopping searches, usually yes, because category pages match the intent of shoppers who are still comparing. Product pages rank for specific searches like model names. A strong store optimises both, but category pages often bring the most organic traffic.

### How much text should a category page have?

Enough to help shoppers, not to hit a word count. A short introduction of two or three sentences above the products, and a few hundred words of genuinely useful buying advice or FAQs below them, works well for most categories.

### Should filter pages be indexed by Google?

Only when a filter combination matches real search demand and has enough products. Most filter combinations, like a price range plus a colour plus a size, should not be indexed. Google's faceted navigation guide recommends controlling these URLs so they don't waste crawling.

### What is the difference between a collection and a category?

They are the same idea with different names. Shopify calls product groupings "collections"; WooCommerce and most other platforms call them "categories". The SEO approach is the same for both.

### Should I delete out-of-stock categories?

Usually not. If a category is seasonal or temporarily empty, keep the URL live, explain availability and link to related categories. Deleting and recreating it each year throws away its history and any links it earned.

### How do I find keywords for category pages?

Start with how customers describe the product, then check volumes in a keyword tool or Google Keyword Planner. Look at the search results: if category pages from other stores rank, the keyword suits a category page. Use autocomplete and "related searches" for subcategory ideas.
